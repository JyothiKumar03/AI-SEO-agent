PRACTICAL MOVES

Creating flow between sentences

Instead of thinking "I need a transition phrase now," ask:
  - What question does this sentence create in the reader's mind?
  - Does the next sentence answer it?

Example:
  "ISO 42001 covers AI systems."
  ↓ Reader asks: "What does it cover about them?"
  "It sets requirements for governing behavior across design, deployment, and monitoring."

The connection is implicit in the idea. You don't need "which is why" or "the result is."

Sometimes you DO need explicit bridges—"but," "however," "that's why"—when the logical 
connection isn't obvious. Use them when clarity demands it, not on schedule.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Deciding when to explain mechanisms

Ask: "Would understanding HOW this works change what the reader does?"

YES → Explain it
  Example: How deep tissue differs from Swedish massage (affects their choice)

NO → State the outcome and move on
  Example: How ISO standards get published (doesn't affect their certification decision)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Using examples well

Don't insert examples because you're "supposed to." Add them when:
  - A concept is abstract and needs grounding
  - The reader might misunderstand without seeing a real case
  - You're contrasting two similar things that are easy to confuse

Examples should feel like they belong there, not like they were bolted on afterward.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Creating emphasis naturally

Emphasis comes from:
  - Sentence length: Short sentences grab attention.
  - Paragraph breaks: New paragraph signals "new important point."
  - Repetition: "Not the other stuff. This."
  - Direct moments: "Here's what matters."

Not from randomly bolding words or adding "IMPORTANT:" tags everywhere.

Tone and voice choices

**Third person / objective tone** works when:
  - Topic is formal (legal, academic, certain business contexts)
  - Audience expects professional distance
  - Content is explanatory but not prescriptive

**Mixed approach** works when:
  - Intro/conclusion address reader directly
  - Body content stays objective for credibility
  - Topic has both educational and decision-making elements

Read the context—audience, brand voice, topic formality—and match your tone to what

Mechanical bridging:
